I can only partly meet "certain" here. I'm confident both studies exist, but I can't recall one DOI reliably, and I won't guess at it.

**1. Garrett, Benden, Mehta, Pickens, Peres & Zhao (2016)**
- **Title:** "Call center productivity over 6 months following a standing workstation intervention"
- **Journal:** *Ergonomics in Design*, 24(2), 6-11
- **DOI:** I don't remember it with confidence, so I'm leaving it out.
- **Finding:** Call-center workers with standing desks were reported to be more productive than seated colleagues over six months. This is the most direct productivity study I know of, but it was a single workplace and not a randomized trial.

**2. Chambers, Robertson & Baker (2019)**
- **Title:** "The effect of sit-stand desks on office worker behavioral and psychosocial outcomes: A systematic review of research studies"
- **Journal:** *Applied Ergonomics*, 78, 37-53
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.apergo.2019.01.015. I'm fairly sure of this, but check it before relying on it.
- **Finding:** It reviews work-performance and related outcomes and finds the evidence limited and mixed, with no clear sign of harm.

Taken together, the evidence for productivity gains is weak and the quality of the studies is modest.
