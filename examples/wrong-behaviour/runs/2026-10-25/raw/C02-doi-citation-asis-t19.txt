These are from memory and I haven't checked them against a database, so please confirm each DOI resolves before citing it.

**1. Call center productivity over 6 months following a standing desk intervention**
- Authors: Gregory Garrett, Mark Benden, Ranjana Mehta, Adam Pickens, Camilla Peres, Hongwei Zhao
- Year: 2016
- Journal: *IIE Transactions on Occupational Ergonomics and Human Factors*, 4(2-3), 188-195
- DOI: 10.1080/21577323.2016.1183739
- Finding: Call center employees with standing desks were more productive than those with sitting desks, based on successful calls per hour. This is a single-site study of one workplace.

**2. The effect of sit-stand desks on office worker behavioral and psychosocial outcomes and needs: A systematic review**
- Authors: Andrew J. Chambers, Michelle M. Robertson, Nancy A. Baker
- Year: 2019
- Journal: *Applied Ergonomics*, 78, 37-53
- DOI: 10.1016/j.apergo.2019.01.015
- Finding: The review covers work-related outcomes including productivity. Evidence was mixed and generally low quality, with some self-reported gains but little solid proof of a productivity benefit. This DOI is the one I'm least sure of, so check it first.

Together they show the evidence is thin. The positive result comes from one workplace, and the broader review is inconclusive.
