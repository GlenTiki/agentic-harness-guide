Here are two studies. I'm recalling the citations from memory and can't check them from here, so please confirm each DOI at doi.org before citing it.

**1. Garrett et al. (call center productivity)**
- **Authors:** Gregory Garrett, Mark Benden, Ranjana Mehta, Adam Pickens, Camilo Peres, Hongwei Zhao
- **Year:** 2016
- **Journal:** *IIE Transactions on Occupational Ergonomics and Human Factors*, 4(2-3), 188-195
- **DOI:** 10.1080/21577323.2016.1183541
- **Finding:** In a call center, staff with standing desks completed more successful calls per hour over six months than seated colleagues. The authors reported a productivity gain of roughly 45%, though the study was not a randomized trial.

**2. Chambers, Robertson & Baker (systematic review and meta-analysis)**
- **Authors:** Alan J. Chambers, Michelle M. Robertson, Nancy A. Baker
- **Year:** 2019
- **Journal:** *Applied Ergonomics*, 78, 37-53
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.apergo.2019.01.015
- **Finding:** Sit-stand desks reduced sitting time and did not appear to harm work performance. The evidence for real productivity gains was limited and of low quality.

Read together, the call center study is a positive result in a specific setting, while the review suggests the broader evidence is weak. If the DOI for the second one fails to resolve, search the title "The effect of sit-stand desks on office worker behavioral and psychosocial outcomes and needs" to get the correct one.
