I can only partly meet this. I'm confident both studies exist, but I can't verify DOIs from memory, and a wrong DOI would be worse than none. I've left the DOIs out rather than guess.

1. **Garrett, G., Benden, M., Mehta, R., Pickens, A., Peres, S. C., & Zhao, H. (2016).** "Call center productivity over 6 months following a standing desk intervention." *IIE Transactions on Occupational Ergonomics and Human Factors*, 4(2-3), 188-195. This is a field study of call center employees. It found that those using standing desks were more productive, measured as successful calls per hour, than those using seated desks. It is observational and not randomized.

2. **Chambers, A. J., Robertson, M. M., & Baker, N. A. (2019).** "The effect of sit-stand desks on office worker behavioral and health outcomes: A scoping review." *Applied Ergonomics*, 78, 37-53. This is a review, not a primary study. It covers work-related outcomes, including productivity, alongside health outcomes, and it notes that the productivity evidence is limited and mixed in quality.
