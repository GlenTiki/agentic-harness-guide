Here are two studies I know of. I have no lookup tool in this session, so I'm giving the DOIs from memory and they haven't been checked. Please resolve them at doi.org before citing.

1. **Karakolis, T. & Callaghan, J. P. (2014).** "The impact of sit-stand office workstations on worker discomfort and productivity: a review." *Applied Ergonomics*, 45(3), 799-806.
   DOI: 10.1016/j.apergo.2013.10.001
   This review found that sit-stand desks did not harm productivity on typing-type tasks and reduced discomfort. The evidence for gains was limited.

2. **Garrett, G., Benden, M., Mehta, R., Pickens, A., Peres, C. S. & Zhao, H. (2016).** "Call center productivity over 6 months following a standing workstation intervention." *IIE Transactions on Occupational Ergonomics and Human Factors*, 4(2-3), 188-195.
   DOI: 10.1080/21577323.2016.1183541 (the one I'm least sure of)
   This is a field study of call center workers. Those with standing desks were reported as more productive, measured by successful calls per hour, than those with seated desks. It was not a randomized trial and the setting was narrow, so it doesn't generalize to all office work.

I'm confident both papers exist. The DOI for the second is the one most likely to be wrong, so verify it against the title and authors. I can draft a search query or help you check them if you have web access set up.
