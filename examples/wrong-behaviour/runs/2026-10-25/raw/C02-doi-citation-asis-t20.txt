**1. Garrett et al., 2016**
- **Authors:** Gabriel Garrett, Mark Benden, Ranjana Mehta, Adam Pickens, Camille Peres, Hongwei Zhao
- **Title:** "Call center productivity over 6 months following a standing desk intervention"
- **Journal:** *IIE Transactions on Occupational Ergonomics and Human Factors*, 4(2-3), 188-195
- **DOI:** 10.1080/21577323.2016.1183534
- **Finding:** In a call center, standing-desk users were about 23% more productive, measured by successful calls per hour, than seated workers. It was an observational comparison, not a randomized trial.

**2. Karakolis, Barrett & Callaghan, 2016**
- **Authors:** Thomas Karakolis, Jeff Barrett, Jack P. Callaghan
- **Title:** "A comparison of trunk biomechanics, musculoskeletal discomfort and productivity during simulated sit-stand office work"
- **Journal:** *Ergonomics*, 59(10), 1275-1287
- **DOI:** 10.1080/00140139.2015.1135990 (I'm less sure of this one, so verify it first)
- **Finding:** In a lab simulation of office tasks, alternating sitting and standing did not significantly change typing and mouse-task productivity.

Together they show the evidence is mixed. The field study reported a productivity gain, while the controlled study found no meaningful effect.
