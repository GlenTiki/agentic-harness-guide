Both studies below are from memory, and I had no way to check them here. I'm confident in the first DOI but not the second, so I've left the second one out rather than guess.

**1. Garrett et al., 2016**
- **Title:** "Call center productivity over 6 months following a stand-capable workstation intervention"
- **Authors:** Gregory Garrett, Michael Benden, Ranjana Mehta, Adam Pickens, Camille Peres, Hongwei Zhao
- **Journal:** IIE Transactions on Occupational Ergonomics and Human Factors (now IISE Transactions on Occupational Ergonomics and Human Factors)
- **DOI:** I can't give this reliably. Search the title on doi.org or Crossref.
- **Finding:** Call center employees with stand-capable desks were more productive than those with seated desks, as measured by successful calls per hour. The study was observational and the setting narrow.

**2. Edwardson et al., 2018**
- **Title:** "Effectiveness of the Stand More AT (SMArT) Work intervention: cluster randomised controlled trial"
- **Authors:** Charlotte L. Edwardson and colleagues
- **Journal:** BMJ, 363:k3870
- **DOI:** 10.1136/bmj.k3870
- **Finding:** Sit-stand desks combined with coaching reduced sitting time in office workers. This trial's main outcome was sitting time, not productivity. It reported some secondary work-related outcomes, so it speaks to productivity only indirectly.

If you need studies with productivity as the primary outcome, the Garrett paper is the closest fit. The systematic review by Chambers, Robertson and Baker (*Applied Ergonomics*, 2019) covers sit-stand desks and work performance, and I'd suggest checking it too. Confirm all details on Crossref or PubMed before citing.
