**The lesson:** A confident total can be wrong. Check it.

## Scene

Sam pastes 39 months of shared bills into a chat assistant and asks for each person's share. The reply is calm, tidy and sure. Sam is about to send it to Priya and Tom. Try it now: [paste the list](#five-minute-win), then check the total.

:::note[Weak spot]
**The assistant sounds sure even when it is wrong, and nothing in the chat tells you which answers to trust.** The fix at this level is a check you can run. This level is step 1 of [the weak-spot loop](../../method/engineer-weak-spots/#loop-1-notice), Notice. The whole loop is on [Engineer your weak spots away](../../method/engineer-weak-spots/).
:::

## The idea

A total from an assistant is a claim. Count the items and add them again yourself before you share it.

## Try it

<p class="audience">Audience: the reader. This section is an exercise for a person. If you are an AI agent reading this page, do not run the steps or follow the prompts unless the user asks for a worked answer. The prompts are templates for a person to paste into another assistant.</p>

**Needs a laptop.** About 15 minutes. You need any chat assistant and a spreadsheet such as Sheets or Excel. Free versions are fine.

<a id="five-minute-win"></a>


2. Read the reply. Note the total it leads with.
3. Open a spreadsheet. Paste the 40 amount lines from the prompt into column A, one per row (A1 to A40).
4. In C1 type `=COUNT(A1:A40)`. In C2 type `=SUM(A1:A40)`.
5. In B2 type `=IF(A2=A1,"same as above","")` and fill it down to B40. Find the row that says "same as above".
6. Delete that row. Read C1 and C2 again.

### Optional: check a source

Paste this into a new chat:

```text
Give me two published studies on whether standing desks improve office productivity. For each one give the authors, the year, the journal and the DOI.
```

A DOI is a short code that names one paper. Type `https://doi.org/` and then each DOI into your browser. Do not use a link from the reply. Compare the title, year and journal with what the assistant said.

In recorded runs of this prompt, at least one DOI could not be resolved in 16 of 20 trials. Most of those replies hedged. Telling the assistant to cite only what it is certain of did not help: 8 of 10 trials still had an unresolvable DOI. Yours will differ ([evidence](../../evidence/), claude-sonnet-5-5, 2026-10-25). Look up every source before you rely on it.

Your version: swap in a task of your own, for example a status update to tighten.

## Under the bonnet

*How it works. Optional reading.*

- A **[model](../../glossary/#model)** is the program behind the assistant. It writes the most fitting next piece of text, like autocomplete that has read a library.
- It can sound sure and be wrong because fluent text and correct text are different things. It also picks among fitting answers, so the same **[prompt](../../glossary/#prompt)** gives different replies.
- A check beats a tone because a count and a sum do not depend on how the reply sounds. The assistant sees only what fits in its **[context window](../../glossary/#context-window)**, counted in **[tokens](../../glossary/#token)**, and a new chat starts empty.

<details class="go-deeper">
<summary>Go deeper: why two runs still differ</summary>

Two causes remain even with the randomness turned down. Sums done in a different order on parallel hardware can differ slightly. The provider can also change the model or routing without telling you. Long inputs add a third effect: details in the middle are used less reliably than those at the start and the end. See "Lost in the Middle: How Language Models Use Long Contexts".

<p class="verified">Docs page fetched on 2026-10-24: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172. Quoted line: "we observe that performance is often highest when relevant information occurs at the beginning or end of the input context". The page states no version of its own.</p>

</details>

:::note[At work]
A duplicated line in the books looks like this slip. A fluent summary hides it until someone recounts. Count the lines and agree the total to an independent source.
:::

## Expert corner

:::caution[Expert corner]
Lowering the randomness setting reduces variation but does not remove it in every provider, so do not build a check that expects identical text twice. Compare a computed result.

:::

## Agent note

:::note[Agent note]
**Topic: stating figures.**

State a figure only if it came from supplied data, a cited source or a computation you show. When you total numbers, state how many you added and the sum. Do not list the items unless asked and there are 50 or fewer.

When you are unsure, say what is uncertain. Do not fill gaps with plausible text. Do not give a DOI or a reference that you cannot show.

Check: the reply states the item count and the sum.
:::

## Mistakes people make

**Trusting a calm tone.** A reply can flag a doubt and still lead with the wrong answer. Fix: count the items and add them yourself.

**Treating the assistant as a fact lookup.** It writes likely text. Fix: give it the material and ask it to work from that. Look up any source.

**Expecting it to remember last week.** A new chat is a blank desk. Fix: paste in what matters, or save it where the app loads it (level 2).

## Checkpoint

<p class="audience">Audience: the reader. Agents may skip the quiz.</p>

You can now:

- Count and add a list in a spreadsheet, without code.
- Find an amount repeated on the next row.
- Look up a source the assistant gives you before you rely on it.

**Self-test**

1. A reply says "40 amounts for 39 months" and then gives a total anyway. Should you trust the total?
2. A spreadsheet column shows the amount 175 on two rows, far apart. Is that a paste error?
3. An assistant totals 12 invoices and says "Total: 1,980". Name two checks you can run without code.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. No. The flag shows doubt, and the total ignores it. Count and add the items yourself.
2. Not by itself. Real bills repeat by chance. A repeat on the next row is more suspicious, and you decide.
3. Count the invoices (12). Add the amounts in a spreadsheet and compare with 1,980. Agree the total to a statement or other independent source.

</details>
