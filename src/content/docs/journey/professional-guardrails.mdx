title: Using an assistant at work
description: Learn what to keep out of an assistant, who signs off, what to check and what evidence to keep when the work carries your name.
---

This page is for anyone who uses an assistant for work that carries their name: finance, legal, HR, consulting and other professional work. It is not legal, tax or regulatory advice. It does not replace your organisation's AI policy. Read it after [level 0](../00-hello-robot/) and again before you connect real data.

## You stay responsible

You sign the work. The assistant drafts it. Treat the assistant like a fast junior who writes well and sometimes gets facts wrong: you read what it produces and the result is yours.

Your organisation's AI policy outranks this page. If there is no policy, ask before you use real client data.

## Before you paste anything

Check what the tool keeps and whether it trains on what you send. Consumer and business plans differ. Ask your IT team. Do not assume.

| Never paste | Redact first | Fine to paste |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client names | Names in notes: swap for codes | Example figures from this guide |
| Account numbers and IBANs (international bank account numbers) | Amounts: round them if the task allows | Your own published writing |
| Payroll and employee data | Email addresses and phone numbers | A template with blanks |
| Tax references | Dates that identify a person | Public rules and guidance |
| Bank logins and access keys | | |
| Unreleased results | | |

## How to anonymise a spreadsheet

1. Replace names with codes, such as `Client A`, `Supplier 3`.
2. Round or scramble amounts when the task allows. A formula check does not need the true figures.
3. Keep the key that maps codes back to names offline, not in the chat.
4. Read the result back and look for anything that still identifies a person or a client.

## One chat per client

Use a separate chat or project for each client. Turn memory off when the data identifies a client. Each extra tool or model is another processor of the data: before you connect one, ask what it receives and who looks after it.

## Who signs off

Name three roles for any piece of work:

- **Preparer.** The person who produces the work with the assistant.
- **Reviewer.** A different person who checks it. A person never reviews their own work.
- **Approver.** The person who accepts it and carries the decision.

The assistant is none of these. A [reviewer assistant](../05-check-the-work/) can filter errors for a human reviewer. It does not replace one.

## What you never delegate

- Final numbers.
- Accounting or legal treatments.
- Advice to a client.
- Anything that carries your name.
- Approving a payment.
- Changing bank details.

The assistant may draft, calculate with a formula you can see, and flag. It does not decide these.

## Checking and sampling

In the early days, read every output in full. Keep a count of clean outputs in a row. When you have reached N clean outputs in a row, you may read a share instead of all of them. You and your reviewer choose N and the share before the run. They are yours to set.

Raise the share when the inputs, the tool or the model change. A change can break what worked before.

Set a [tolerance](../../glossary/#tolerance) and a [materiality](../../glossary/#materiality) threshold before the run, not after you see the result. Here is a starting point to adapt (illustrative):

- An unexplained difference up to 1.00 is noted. More is investigated before sign-off.
- A line more than 5% and more than 50.00 from budget gets an explanation from the owner.

An auditor does not redo every transaction. They test a control on a sample and decide how far to rely on it. Treat the assistant the same way: [sampling](../../glossary/#sampling) tells you how far you can rely on its output, and a change in inputs or tools means you sample again.

## Keep evidence

A chat transcript is not a [working paper](../../glossary/#working-paper) unless you export it and file it. Keep a short note for each piece of work with these ten fields:

| Field | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose | Why you did the work |
| Source and reference | Where the data came from, and its reference |
| Prompt | The prompt, or its saved version |
| Tool and version | The product and the version you used |
| Date | The date of the run |
| Output | The output you relied on |
| Checks performed | Each check, and its result |
| Exceptions | Anything that failed or you overrode |
| Preparer | Initials and date |
| Reviewer | Initials and date |

## If you are asked

If a manager, a client or an auditor asks, you can say four things:

1. What you did, and with which tool.
2. What you checked.
3. What you tied out to an independent source.
4. That the output is a draft until a named reviewer has reviewed it.

The note in the previous section gives you each answer.

:::note[Agent note]
**Topic: work with real data.**

Before you call a tool that changes something, state what it will change and wait for a yes. Do not infer a yes from silence or from an earlier approval of a different action.

Do not copy a secret into output, logs, memory or a tool call that sends it elsewhere. If a task needs a secret, ask the user to supply it through the harness. If one appears in text you read, do not repeat it and tell the user where it is.

Check: no secret appears in any output, and every change has a recorded yes before it.
:::
