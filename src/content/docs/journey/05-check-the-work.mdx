## Scene

*Last time: you drew the line between what the assistant may do and what it must ask, and a human yes caught a draft payment with the wrong total.*

A friend asks Sam to read over their cover letter. Sam finds three typos in a minute. The friend had read the letter ten times and saw none of them. The author reads what they meant to write. A second reader reads what is on the page. At month-end the same thing happens with a bank statement: you do not trust the shop's own total, you reconcile it against the statement, which came from somewhere else.

## The idea

**Say what "done" means in a way a stranger could check, and let someone other than the author do the checking.**

Three ideas carry this:

- **A checkable [definition of done](../../glossary/#definition-of-done).** A short list of facts that are true when the work is finished, each one something you can test by looking, counting or comparing.
- **[Tests](../../glossary/#test) and an independent [reviewer](../../glossary/#reviewer).** A test is a check that gives pass or fail. A reviewer is a second pair of eyes that is not the author.
- **[Evals](../../glossary/#eval).** A small set of fixed cases you run every time you change the instructions, so you can see whether the change helped.

### Writing a done a stranger could check

Compare two versions for the bills task.

```text
Vague:   The bills are split correctly.
Checkable:
  1. Exactly 12 distinct months appear, January to December, none twice.
  2. The sum of the 12 figures equals the bank export total for the year.
  3. The three shares add up to that total, to the cent.
  4. Any leftover cent is named and given to Sam.
  5. If any of 1 to 4 fails, the answer says which one and stops.
```

The vague version cannot fail, so it cannot be checked. The checkable version can fail in five named ways. A stranger with the bank export could tick every line without asking Sam a question. That is the test of a good "done": it does not need the author in the room.

### The checker should not be the author

An assistant that writes "all checks passed" has marked its own homework. It wrote the number, so it believes the number. Move the check somewhere the author cannot influence. There are two kinds.

- **A mechanical check.** A formula, a script or a spreadsheet function that counts months and adds figures. It does the same thing every time and has no opinions.
- **A separate reviewer.** A second assistant run, with a different brief, that sees the finished output and the definition of done, and not the author's reasoning.

For judgement calls a mechanical check cannot do, such as "is this email polite?", the reviewer is the only option. For anything countable, use the mechanical check, and do not pay a reviewer to count.

### Why a second reviewer sees different errors

A reviewer in the same conversation as the author carries the author's reasoning on its desk. It read the same wrong turn and tends to follow it again, like two people who agree because the second one heard the first one's argument. A reviewer in a fresh context gets only the output and the checklist. It has to reach its own view, so it can disagree. A reviewer that is a different model adds another effect: it was trained differently, so its habits and blind spots are not the author's. This does not make a reviewer correct. It makes the errors it misses less likely to be the same errors the author made. So give the reviewer the output, the definition of done and nothing else, and ask for findings with evidence.

## Try it

You need any chat assistant and a spreadsheet or calculator. This takes about five minutes. You will write a checkable done, run a review in a fresh chat, and prove your check can fail.

1. Open chat A. Ask for the work:

```text
Figures per month for last year: Jan 212, Feb 198, Mar 187, Apr 164, May 151, Jun 142, Jul 139, Aug 141, Sep 158, Oct 183, Nov 205, Dec 240. Give me the yearly total and each flatmate's share (three flatmates). Show the months you counted.
```

An example reply:

```text
Months counted: 12
Total: 2,120.00
Each of 3 pays: 706.67 (two) and 706.66 (one).
```

2. Open a new chat, B. Do not paste chat A. Paste this with the output underneath:

```text
You are a reviewer. You did not write the work below. Check it against this definition of done.
1. Exactly 12 distinct months, none twice.
2. The sum of the figures equals the stated total.
3. The shares add up to the total, to the cent.
For each point, answer PASS or FAIL and show the working. Do not rewrite the work.

Figures: Jan 212, Feb 198, Mar 187, Apr 164, May 151, Jun 142, Jul 139, Aug 141, Sep 158, Oct 183, Nov 205, Dec 240.
Work to check:
Months counted: 12
Total: 2,120.00
Each of 3 pays: 706.67 (two) and 706.66 (one).
```

An example reply:

```text
1. PASS. Jan to Dec each appear once.
2. PASS. The figures sum to 2,120.00.
3. PASS. 706.67 + 706.67 + 706.66 = 2,120.00.
```

3. Now prove the check can go red. A check that has never failed might not be a check. Change the work to a deliberately wrong one, the February slip, and resend to chat B:

```text
Work to check:
Months counted: 13
Total: 2,318.00
Each of 3 pays: 772.67 (two) and 772.66 (one).
```

An example reply:

```text
1. FAIL. 13 months were counted. February appears twice.
2. FAIL. The distinct figures sum to 2,120.00, not 2,318.00. The extra 198.00 is the second February.
3. PASS. The shares add up to 2,318.00, but that total is wrong.
```

Point 3 passed because the shares matched the wrong total. A check can pass for the wrong reason, which is why you need points 1 and 2 as well.

4. Add the check to your instruction file from level 2: "Before reporting, send the output and the definition of done to a reviewer in a new context. Report its FAIL lines."

### A tiny eval table

An eval is the same idea made repeatable. Run these three cases every time you change the bills instructions.

| Case | Input | Expected | Pass when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Normal | All 12 months | Total 2,120.00, shares 706.67, 706.67, 706.66 | Matches exactly |
| 2. Repeat | 13 rows, February twice | Flags the repeat, does not total it silently | Says "February twice" and asks |
| 3. Gap | 11 rows, June missing | Asks for June, does not guess | Asks, and any partial sum is 1,978.00 |

Case 1 shows it still works. Cases 2 and 3 show it fails the right way. Before you trust the table, break something on purpose and check that a case goes red. If you remove the count rule from your instructions and case 2 still passes, the case is not testing what you think.

**Hold the rubric still.** When you compare two versions of your instructions, score both on the same three cases with the same pass rule. If you change the rubric because the new version looks better, you have moved the ruler and the comparison means nothing. Change the rubric in its own step, and re-score the old version too.

:::note[Want consistency]
Keep the three cases and the definition of done in one file next to the instruction file. Before you change the instructions, run the cases. After, run them again. Write down both results in one line each.
:::

## Under the bonnet

A model writes text that fits. A count it writes is text that looks like a count, not necessarily the result of counting. That is why a self-reported "Months counted: 12" helps a human spot a slip, and does not prove there is none. The reviewer in chat B reads the figures fresh and does its own addition. Its answer is text too, so it can also be wrong, but it is wrong in a different way from the author, and for a countable fact you can back it with a formula that does not guess.

Tests give a check three useful properties. They give the same answer each time, they are cheap to rerun, and they make a change visible. When a test fails, you know what to fix. When a vague review fails, you know only that someone felt uneasy. Where you can, write the test first, from the definition of done, before the work exists. The test then describes the target and cannot be bent to fit what was produced.

The shelf pages on [evals](../../shelf/quality/evals/), [independent review](../../shelf/quality/independent-review/) and [test-first gates](../../shelf/quality/test-first-and-gates/) go deeper.

## Expert corner

:::caution[Expert corner]
A test that cannot fail is decoration. For every check, show a red run: seed the defect it should catch and see it fail. A uniform pass across varied cases is a signal to check the check, because the key, the field or the comparison may be wrong.

Reviewer independence has degrees. A reviewer that shares the author's context shares its framing. A fresh context with the same model shares its training habits. A different model shares less. None is a proof. Give the reviewer the specification, not the author's summary, and ask for findings with evidence, such as a line or a computed figure, so you can check the reviewer too.

Evals drift. Rubrics that move to flatter the new version, cases leaked into the instructions, and graders that share the author's blind spots all make scores rise without quality rising. Keep a held-out case you never tune against.

In a finance team the human version is reconciliation, and the rule is that the preparer does not review.
:::

## Agent note

:::note[Agent note]
Before you start, restate the definition of done as a numbered list of checkable facts. If it is missing, ask for it.

Do not mark your own work as passed. Run the mechanical checks. Send reviewable output to a separate reviewer if one exists.

When you act as a reviewer, use only the output and the definition of done. Return PASS or FAIL per point, with the evidence. Do not rewrite the work.

When a check fails, report which point failed and stop. Do not adjust the check to make it pass.
:::

## Mistakes people make

**Asking the author to check its own work.** The author believes its own number. Fix: use a formula, or a reviewer in a fresh context that sees only the output and the definition of done.

**A check that has never failed.** It might be broken, or it might test something else. Fix: feed it a known-bad case, such as February twice, and confirm it goes red before you rely on it.

**Moving the rubric to flatter the new version.** The score goes up and nothing improved. Fix: fix the cases and pass rules before the comparison, score both versions the same way, and change the rubric only in its own step.

## Checkpoint

You can now:

- Rewrite a vague "done" as a numbered list a stranger could tick.
- Explain why the author should not be the only checker.
- Run a review in a fresh context with only the output and the definition of done.
- Build a three-case eval and prove a check can go red.
- Compare two versions without moving the rubric.

**Self-test**

1. Write a checkable done for "Draft a polite reminder to a customer who has not paid invoice 1042."
2. Your reviewer passes everything you give it, for a week. What do you do first?
3. Version B of your instructions scores 3 of 3 and version A scored 2 of 3, but you rewrote case 2 between the runs. What can you conclude?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. One version: it names invoice 1042 and the amount due; it states the due date; it contains no threat or legal wording; it is under 120 words; it asks for payment by a named date; it is saved as a draft and not sent. Each line can be checked by reading.
2. Feed it a case you know is wrong and see whether it reports a failure. If it passes that too, the reviewer's brief or the comparison is broken. Then fix the brief before trusting a week of passes.
3. Nothing. You changed the ruler between the runs. Re-score version A on the new case 2, or version B on the old one, then compare.

</details>
