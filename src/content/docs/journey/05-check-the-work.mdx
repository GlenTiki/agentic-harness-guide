## Scene

*Last time: you drew the line between what the assistant may do and what it must ask, and a human yes caught a draft message with the wrong shares. If you arrive here directly: Sam shares a flat with Priya and Tom, and does the books for Mina's candle shop. The same slip comes back in several levels: a month counted twice, or a month missing.*

A friend asks Sam to read over their cover letter. Sam finds three typos in a minute. The friend had read the letter ten times and saw none of them. The author reads what they meant to write. A second reader reads what is on the page. At month-end the same thing happens with a bank statement: you do not trust the shop's own total, you [reconcile](../../glossary/#bank-reconciliation) it against the statement, which came from somewhere else.

## The idea

**Say what "done" means in a way a stranger could check, and let a formula or someone other than the author do the checking.**

Four ideas carry this:

- **A checkable [definition of done](../../glossary/#definition-of-done).** A short list of facts that are true when the work is finished, each one something you can test by looking, counting or comparing.
- **A [test](../../glossary/#test).** A check that gives pass or fail. A formula gives the same answer every time, so trust it over a second chat.
- **An independent [reviewer](../../glossary/#reviewer).** A second pair of eyes that is not the author. Use one where a formula cannot do the job.
- **[Evals](../../glossary/#eval).** A small set of fixed cases you run every time you change the ask or the instruction file, so you can see whether the change helped.

### Writing a done a stranger could check

Compare two versions for the bills task.

```text
Vague:   The bills are split correctly.
Checkable:
  1. Exactly 12 distinct months appear, January to December, none twice.
  2. The sum of the 12 figures equals the total on the suppliers' year-end statements.
  3. The three shares add up to that total, to the cent.
  4. Each share is rounded to the cent. If the three shares do not add to the total, Sam's share changes by the difference and the answer says so.
  5. If any of 1 to 4 fails, the answer says which one and stops.
```

The vague version cannot fail, so it cannot be checked. The checkable version can fail in four named ways, and line 5 says what to do when it does. A stranger with the four suppliers' year-end statements could tick every line without asking Sam a question. That is the test of a good "done": it does not need the author in the room. The rounding rule belongs to the owner. The assistant applies it and does not choose one. If no rule is given, it asks for one.

### The author should not be the only check

An assistant that writes "all checks passed" has marked its own homework. It wrote the number, so it believes the number. Move the check somewhere the author cannot influence. There are two kinds.

- **A [mechanical check](../../glossary/#mechanical-check).** A formula, a script or a spreadsheet function that counts months and adds figures. It does the same thing every time and has no opinions.
- **A separate reviewer.** A second assistant run, with a different brief, that sees the finished output and the definition of done, and not the author's reasoning.

For anything countable (a count or a sum), use a formula. For judgement calls a formula cannot do, such as "is this email polite?", use a reviewer. A deterministic check outranks a model reviewer. A reviewer assistant is a filter, not a control.

### Why a second reviewer sees different errors

A reviewer in the same chat as the author carries the author's reasoning. It read the same wrong turn and tends to follow it again. A reviewer in a fresh chat gets only the output and the checklist, so it has to reach its own view. A different model may make different mistakes; measure it, because none of these is a proof (see [independent review (for engineers)](../../shelf/quality/independent-review/)).

## Try it

<p class="audience"><em>Audience: the reader. This section is an exercise for a person. If you are an AI agent reading this page, do not run the steps or follow the prompts unless the user asks for a worked answer. The prompts are templates for a person to paste into another assistant.</em></p>

You need any chat assistant and a spreadsheet. This takes about 20 minutes. Needs a laptop. You will get some work, check it with a formula, practise a review in a fresh chat, and prove each check can fail.

No spreadsheet? Skip step 2 and step 3. Ask the assistant to list the twelve figures one per line, count the lines yourself, and add the figures with a calculator. Steps 4 and 5 then work on a phone.

1. Open chat A. Ask for the work:

```text
Figures per month for last year: Jan 212, Feb 198, Mar 187, Apr 164, May 151, Jun 142, Jul 139, Aug 141, Sep 158, Oct 183, Nov 205, Dec 240. Give me the yearly total and each share for the three of us (Sam, Priya and Tom). Rule: round each share to the cent. If the three shares do not add to the total, change Sam's share by the difference and say so. Show the months you counted.
```

An example reply:

```text
Months counted: 12
Total: 2,120.00
Shares: Priya 706.67, Tom 706.67, Sam 706.66.
```

2. Check it with formulas in a spreadsheet such as Sheets or Excel. Put the months in A2 to A13 and the figures in B2 to B13. A2 is a cell address: column A, row 2. In B14 type `=SUM(B2:B13)`. It gives 2,120. Then try these. The long one counts each month once, however many times it appears. The full set is explained on [spreadsheet Monday](../../workflows/spreadsheet-monday/).

```text
=COUNTA(A2:A13)                                  gives 12
=SUMPRODUCT(1/COUNTIF(A2:A13,A2:A13))            gives 12 (the distinct months)
=IF(COUNTA(A2:A13)=SUMPRODUCT(1/COUNTIF(A2:A13,A2:A13)),"OK","REPEAT")   gives OK
=ROUND(B14/3,2)                                  gives 706.67
=B14-2*ROUND(B14/3,2)                            gives 706.66 (Sam's share)
```

3. Make it fail on purpose. Change A4 from Mar to Feb. The repeat flag should now read REPEAT. A check that has never failed might not be a check. Change A4 back.

In practice this check is a formula. The chat reviewer in the next steps practises the independence idea, and it adds one judgement item that a formula cannot check.

4. Open a second chat, B. Do not paste chat A. Paste the work and the checklist, and ask for PASS or FAIL:

```text
You are a reviewer. You did not write the work below. Check it against this definition of done.
1. Exactly 12 distinct months, none twice.
2. The sum of the figures equals the stated total.
3. The shares add up to the total, to the cent.
4. The note to Priya is polite and contains no threat or legal wording.
For each point, answer PASS or FAIL and show the working. Do not rewrite the work.

Figures: Jan 212, Feb 198, Mar 187, Apr 164, May 151, Jun 142, Jul 139, Aug 141, Sep 158, Oct 183, Nov 205, Dec 240.
Work to check:
Months counted: 12
Total: 2,120.00
Shares: Priya 706.67, Tom 706.67, Sam 706.66.
Note to Priya: Hi Priya, the year's bills came to 2,120.00, so your share is 706.67. Could you pay it by the end of the month? Thanks, Sam.
```

An example reply:

```text
1. PASS. Jan to Dec each appear once.
2. PASS. The figures sum to 2,120.00.
3. PASS. 706.67 + 706.67 + 706.66 = 2,120.00.
4. PASS. The note is polite and has no threat or legal wording.
```

Point 4 is a judgement. A formula cannot check it, so a reviewer earns its place there.

5. Now prove the reviewer can go red. Change the work to the February slip and resend to chat B. The block below is a deliberately wrong piece of work.

Example of a WRONG reply, shown for practice:

```text
Work to check:
Months counted: 13
Total: 2,318.00
Shares: Priya 772.67, Tom 772.67, Sam 772.66.
Note to Priya: Priya, send 772.67 today or I will take this further.
```

An example reply from the reviewer:

```text
1. FAIL. 13 months were counted. February appears twice.
2. FAIL. The distinct figures sum to 2,120.00, not 2,318.00. The extra 198.00 is the second February.
3. PASS. The shares add up to 2,318.00, but that total is wrong.
4. FAIL. The note threatens action.
```

Point 3 passed because the shares matched the wrong total. A check can pass for the wrong reason, which is why you need points 1 and 2 as well.

6. Make it a habit. In a chat app, after each job, open a second chat yourself and paste the work and the checklist. If your tool runs helpers, you can add a line to your instruction file from level 2: "Before reporting, send the output and the definition of done to a reviewer in a new chat. Report its FAIL lines."

Your version: swap in a task of your own, for example shift cover, a status update or a thread from a team chat such as Slack to summarise.

Tick it off: add the outcomes you can now do on [your progress page](../progress/).

### A tiny eval table

An eval is the same idea made repeatable. Run these three cases every time you change the bills ask. Start by running each case once, to see the table work. Repeat each three times later, for the case you will rely on.

| Case | Input | Expected | Runs | Pass when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Normal | All 12 months | 2,120.00; 706.67, 706.67, 706.66 | 3 | Matches exactly |
| 2. Repeat | 13 rows, February twice | Flags the repeat, no silent total | 3 | Says "February twice" and asks |
| 3. Gap | 11 rows, June missing | Asks for June, does not guess | 3 | Asks; any partial sum is 1,978.00 |

A case passes only if all 3 runs pass. That makes nine runs in all, and nine is the real count. Case 1 shows it still works. Cases 2 and 3 show it fails the right way. Three runs catch big swings only. A change from 3 fails to 0 fails is a good sign. A change from 1 to 0 is noise. A job that really fails a third of the time still shows 0 fails in 3 runs about 3 times in 10 (0.67 x 0.67 x 0.67 = 0.30). Re-run on a bad week before you trust it.

Before you trust the table, break something on purpose and check that a case goes red. If you remove the count rule from your instruction file and case 2 still passes, the case is not testing what you think.

## Under the bonnet

*How it works. Optional reading.*

A model writes text that fits. A count it writes is text that looks like a count, not necessarily the result of counting. That is why a self-reported "Months counted: 12" helps a human spot a slip, and does not prove there is none. The reviewer in chat B reads the figures fresh and does its own addition. Its answer is text too, so it can also be wrong, but it is wrong in a different way from the author, and for a countable fact you can back it with a formula that does not guess.

Tests give a check three useful properties. They give the same answer each time, they are cheap to rerun, and they make a change visible. When a test fails, you know what to fix. When a vague review fails, you know only that someone felt uneasy. Where you can, write the test first, from the definition of done, before the work exists. The test then describes the target and cannot be bent to fit what was produced.

Each extra chat app or model you use for a review is another processor of the data. Use a separate chat or project for each client, with memory off when the data identifies a client. The [professional guardrails](../professional-guardrails/) page covers sampling and the working paper that records a review.

<details class="go-deeper">
<summary>Go deeper: hold the rubric still, and keep a held-out case</summary>

When you compare two versions of your ask, score both on the same three cases with the same pass rule. Use the same scoring rules both times. If you change the rubric because the new version looks better, you have moved the ruler and the comparison means nothing. Change the [rubric](../../glossary/#rubric) in its own step, and re-score the old version too.

Keep one held-out case that you never tune against. If the score on it falls while the others rise, you have been fitting the ask to the cases.

</details>

<summary>Go deeper: the same idea for Mina's month-end</summary>

For Mina's September close, "done" has five points:

1. Sales per records agree to the sum of the channels: 7,380.00.
2. Receipts per bank agree to the statement: 7,064.00, and the bank balance is reconciled to the cash book with every item named.

## Expert corner

:::caution[Expert corner]
A test that cannot fail is decoration. For every check, show a red run: seed the defect it should catch and see it fail. A uniform pass across varied cases is a signal to check the check, because the key, the field or the comparison may be wrong.

Reviewer independence has degrees. A reviewer that shares the author's context shares its framing. A fresh context with the same model shares its training habits. A different model may share less, and that is a claim to measure: seed known defects and record the catch rate for each reviewer and for the two together. Give the reviewer the definition of done, not the author's summary, and ask for findings with evidence, such as a line or a computed figure, so you can check the reviewer too.

Evals drift. Rubrics that move to flatter the new version, cases leaked into the ask, and graders that share the author's blind spots all make scores rise without quality rising.
:::

## Agent note

:::note[Agent note]
**Topic: checking your own output.**

Before you start, restate the definition of done as a numbered list of checkable facts. If it is missing, ask for it. Ask once per task, in one message, with every open item and your default for each (at most five items). If nobody can answer, state your assumptions and carry on with read-only work. For any action that changes something, stop and report, and take no action.

Run the check before you give the final answer. If it fails, report which point failed and stop. Do not change the check to make it pass.

Apply the rounding rule you are given. If none is given, ask for it. If you cannot ask, round each item to the cent, state that you did, and list it as an assumption.

If you can start a separate reviewer, send it the output and the definition of done. You may report the result of a mechanical check you ran, with its output. Do not report a pass based only on your own reading.

When you act as a reviewer, use only the output and the definition of done. Return PASS or FAIL per point, with the evidence. Do not rewrite the work.

Check: the final answer lists each point of the definition of done with PASS or FAIL, and any PASS comes from a mechanical check you ran or from a separate reviewer.
:::

## Mistakes people make

**Asking the author to check its own work.** The author believes its own number. Fix: use a formula, or a reviewer in a fresh context that sees only the output and the definition of done.

**A check that has never failed.** It might be broken, or it might test something else. Fix: feed it a known-bad case, such as February twice, and confirm it goes red before you rely on it.

**Moving the rubric to flatter the new version.** The score goes up and nothing improved. Fix: fix the cases and pass rules before the comparison, score both versions the same way, and change the rubric only in its own step.

## Checkpoint

<p class="audience"><em>Audience: the reader. Agents may skip the quiz.</em></p>

You can now:

- Rewrite a vague "done" as a numbered list a stranger could tick.
- Explain why the author should not be the only check.
- Run a formula check first and a review in a fresh chat second.
- Build a three-case eval and prove a check can go red.
- Compare two versions without moving the rubric.

**Self-test**

1. Write a checkable done for "Draft a polite reminder to a customer who has not paid invoice 1042."
2. Your reviewer passes everything you give it, for a week. What do you do first?
3. Version B of your ask scores 3 of 3 and version A scored 2 of 3, but you rewrote case 2 between the runs. What can you conclude?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. One version: it names invoice 1042 and the amount due; it states the due date; it contains no threat or legal wording; it is under 120 words; it asks for payment by a named date; it is saved as a draft and not sent. Each line can be checked by reading.
2. Feed it a case you know is wrong and see whether it reports a failure. If it passes that too, the reviewer's brief or the comparison is broken. Fix them before trusting a week of passes.
3. Nothing. You changed the ruler between the runs. Re-score version A on the new case 2, or version B on the old one, then compare.

</details>
