**The lesson:** Give the assistant a goal, constraints, an example and a check step.

## Scene

*Last time: you learned that a model writes likely text, that it varies, and that it only sees what is on its desk. You also checked a total yourself in a spreadsheet. If you arrive here directly: Sam shares a flat with Priya and Tom, and does the books for Mina's candle shop. The same slip comes back in several levels: a month counted twice, or a month missing. The next exercise uses a new, shorter list with the months labelled.*

Suppose Sam types "Make my CV better" and gets back a CV that is longer, shinier and lists a skill Sam does not have. (This is an invented case, not a recorded run.) Sam did not say who would read it, which job it was for or what "better" meant. The assistant guessed. It is a good guesser. It is still guessing.

:::note[Weak spot]
Level 0 left you able to see that a sure-sounding answer can be wrong, but nothing yet makes a wrong answer less likely. **A vague ask gets a confident answer to a question you did not mean.** The fix at this level is a clear ask with constraints and a check step. A script can also lint a plain-English spec, as the [simple English page](../../setup/plain-text-and-simple-english/) shows. This level is step 3 of [the weak-spot loop](../../method/engineer-weak-spots/#loop-3-engineer-a-fix), Engineer a fix.
:::

## The idea

**A good ask removes guesses.** Each part you add takes one guess away.

There are three things and a habit:

- **[Goal](../../glossary/#goal).** What the result is for and who will use it.
- **[Constraints](../../glossary/#constraint).** The limits: what to use, what to avoid, how long, in what shape. The last constraint is the **[check step](../../glossary/#check-step)**: a count or a sum the assistant must write down before it gives a conclusion, and what to do if it fails.
- **[Example](../../glossary/#example).** A small sample of the result you want. Principle, not measured: an example pulls every answer towards its shape, including its length and word choice. Give two examples that differ, or say which features to copy.
- **The habit: the [refinement loop](../../glossary/#refinement-loop).** Ask, read, name what is wrong, ask again. One change per pass.

For numbers, the check is a count and a sum. For writing, ask for a quoted source line for each claim, a word count and `List anything you assumed`. These make a slip easier to spot. You still read the result.

**Bring your own standard.** If you have a style guide, a policy or a checklist, paste it as the constraints.

Here is the CV ask, before and after.

```text
Before:
Make my CV better.

After:
Goal: I am applying for an office administrator job at a small accountancy firm. The reader is a busy partner who will skim for ten seconds.
Constraints: Keep every fact true. Do not invent skills. Use plain words. Keep it to one page. Start each bullet with a past-tense verb.
Example of the style I want for one bullet: "Booked travel and rooms for a team of 12; cut the cost by moving to a single supplier."
Check: Before you answer, list the facts you used from my CV. Tell me anything you assumed.
Here is my CV:
[paste]
```

The second ask is longer. Principle, not measured: stated constraints make it easier to say what you expect and to check the result. Compare the results on a fixed case before you trust a longer ask. Here is a template for any ask:

```text
Goal: [what the result is for]
Reader: [who will use it]
Constraints: [limits, and your standard if you have one]
Example: [a small sample of the shape you want]
Check: State how many [items] you counted and the sum. If the count is not [the count you expect], stop and tell me.
```

:::tip[New to this]
For a quick question, one line is fine. Use the full shape when the result matters, when you will reuse the ask, or when the first answer disappointed you.
:::

## Try it

You need any chat assistant. This takes about 35 minutes in all. The four passes take about 10. You refine one ask in four passes. Use Sam's bills ask, or swap in a task of your own.

<p class="audience">Audience: the reader. This section is an exercise for a person. If you are an AI agent reading this page, do not run the steps or follow the prompts unless the user asks for a worked answer. The prompts are templates for a person to paste into another assistant.</p>

**Phone OK** for passes 1 to 4 and steps 5 and 6. Step 7 needs a laptop with a spreadsheet, and you can skip it.

**The starting ask**, a new exercise with a shorter list and labelled months, with February repeated:

```text
Here are our shared bills for last year, in euros per month: Jan 212, Feb 198, Feb 198, Mar 187, Apr 164, May 151, Jun 142, Jul 139, Aug 141, Sep 158, Oct 183, Nov 205, Dec 240. Split the total between the three of us (Sam, Priya and Tom).
```

**Pass 1: add the goal.** Add this line to the front of your message:

```text
Goal: work out what each of the three of us owes for last year's shared bills, so I can send Priya and Tom one amount each.
```

What changed: the assistant now knows the result is one amount per person. It may still total all 13 figures, or quietly decide that February was a typo. Either way it did not ask you. In recorded runs of the starting ask (claude-sonnet-5-5, 20 trials, 2026-10-25) the reply gave 2,120.00 every time, treating the repeat as a typo and saying so. That is a guess you did not approve. This list is short and the guess was safe. The 40-amount list in level 0 was harder.

**Pass 2: add constraints, with the check step as the last line.** Add these lines under the goal:

```text
Constraints: Use only the figures I gave you. List each month once. If a month is missing or repeated, tell me and ask instead of guessing. Round each share to the cent. If the three shares do not add to the total, change Sam's share by the difference and say so. Keep the answer under ten lines.
Check: Before the final answer, state how many months you counted and the sum of the figures. If the count is not 12, stop and tell me.
```

What changed: the assistant must not invent or repeat figures, must flag problems and must show its count.

**Pass 3: add an example.** Add a tiny sample of the layout, with different figures so the assistant copies the shape and not the numbers:

```text
Layout I want, shown with two made-up months (Jan 100, Feb 50):
Months counted: 2
Total: 150.00
Each of 3 pays: 50.00
```

**Pass 4: assemble the ask.** Put the parts in this order and send them as one message, with the original 13-figure list.

Reply with February once. The assistant should now give the 12-month result: total 2,120.00, shares 706.67, 706.67 and 706.66.

**Step 5: save the ask.** Save it as `bills-ask-v1` in a notes app or a Word document. Put a blank where the expected count goes ("If the count is not [the count you expect]"), so you can reuse it.

**Step 6: run it in three new chats.** Send the assembled ask in each one, then do the same with the starting ask. Write down how many chats did what the constraints said: stop at 13 months and ask. "Better" here means "did what the constraints said", not "gave a total", because a loose ask can give a clean total by guessing. In the recorded runs (20 trials each, 2026-10-25) the starting ask gave 2,120.00 every time. The full ask asked every time. You decide what a repeated month means. The assistant does not. Level 5 builds this into a proper tiny test.

**Step 7: check the sums in a spreadsheet. Optional, needs a spreadsheet such as Sheets or Excel.** Put the months in cells A2 to A13 and the amounts in B2 to B13. In the lines below, the part before the colon names the cell. Type only the formula that starts with `=`.

```text
B14:  =SUM(B2:B13)
B15:  =COUNTA(A2:A13)
B16:  =SUMPRODUCT(1/COUNTIF(A2:A13,A2:A13))
B17:  =IF(B15=B16,"OK","REPEAT")
B18:  =ROUND(B14/3,2)
B19:  =B14-2*ROUND(B14/3,2)
```

B16 counts each different month once, so a repeated month makes B16 smaller than B15. With the corrected list, B14 reads 2,120, B15 and B16 read 12, B17 reads OK, B18 reads 706.67 and B19 reads 706.66. Now add the twelve figures by hand as well. If your hand total, the spreadsheet and the assistant agree, the loop worked. If they do not, copy the mismatch into the chat and say what is wrong. No spreadsheet? Ask the assistant to list the figures one per line, then count the lines yourself.

## Under the bonnet

*How it works. Optional reading.*

The model fills every gap in your ask with its most likely guess. When the guess is wrong, you get a polished answer to a question you did not ask. Each part of the ask closes a different gap:

- The goal closes the gap about purpose.
- The constraints close the gap about limits.
- The example closes the gap about shape, which is hard to describe and easy to show.
- The check step makes a mistake visible, because the model writes its count before its conclusion.

The loop works because one change per pass tells you which change helped.

:::note[At work]
A close checklist works like a good ask. It has a goal, a [tolerance](../../glossary/#tolerance) (how far a figure may differ before someone investigates), a template to fill in and a tick-box. Someone other than the preparer signs it off.
:::

## Expert corner

:::caution[Expert corner]
A check the assistant runs on itself is not an independent check (level 5). In the recorded runs the count was right in 20 of 20 and a stated sum or share was wrong in 10 of 20. A self-reported count helps a human spot a slip. It does not replace an outside test.

State constraints as observable properties, such as "each month appears once", not as intentions, such as "be accurate".
:::

## Agent note

:::note[Agent note]
**Topic: under-specified tasks.**

Ask for the goal, the reader, the constraints and the output format only when the task will produce a deliverable or an action, and the request does not already give them. Skip the questions when the work is small and reversible, or when you can state your assumptions in one line and proceed. For a quick question, answer directly. Ask within the question budget.

Run the check before you give the final answer. If it fails, report which point failed and stop. Do not change the check to make it pass.

Follow any layout example for shape only. Apply the rounding rule you are given. If none is given, ask for it. If you cannot ask, round each item to the cent, state that you did, and list it as an assumption.

When you total numbers, state how many you added and the sum. Do not list the items unless asked and there are 50 or fewer.

Check: the reply shows the count and the sum before the result.
:::

## Mistakes people make

**Changing five things at once.** The answer improves or worsens and you cannot tell why. Fix: change one thing per pass, and note what changed.

**Writing vague constraints.** "Be accurate" and "be professional" cannot be checked. Fix: name something you could verify, such as "list each month once" or "no sentence over 20 words".

**Asking for a check with no plan for a failure.** "Check your work" says nothing about what to do when the check fails. Fix: say what to count, and what to do if it fails.

## Checkpoint

<p class="audience">Audience: the reader. Agents may skip the quiz.</p>

You can now:

- Split an ask into goal, constraints, example and check step.
- Refine an ask in small passes and say what each pass changed.
- Write a constraint that you could check by eye.
- Ask for a count or sum before a conclusion, so a slip shows.

**Self-test**

1. Your first ask is "Write a bio for my website." The answer is bland. Which part of the ask do you add first, and why?
2. You add six constraints at once and the answer gets worse. What went wrong with your method, and what do you do?
3. Rewrite "Summarise this meeting" so it has a goal, one checkable constraint and a check step.

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The goal: who reads the bio and what they should do after reading it. Blandness comes from the assistant guessing a generic reader. An example of the tone you like also helps once the goal is set.
2. You changed too much at once, so you cannot tell which constraint hurt. Go back to the last good version and add one constraint at a time.
3. One version: "Goal: I could not attend and need to know what I must do. Constraints: no more than five bullets; each action names an owner. Check: before the summary, list the decisions you found and say how many; if there are none, say so." Yours may differ if it has all three parts and a checkable limit.

</details>
