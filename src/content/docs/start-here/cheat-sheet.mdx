## The Good Ask card

Put four things in every ask that matters.

- **Goal.** What the result is for and who will use it.
- **Limits.** What to use, what to avoid, how long, in what shape.
- **Example.** A small sample of the result you want.
- **Check.** How the assistant proves it did the job, for example a count and a list of what it assumed.

## The three boxes

Before the assistant touches anything, sort each action into one box.

- **May do.** Safe without asking. Reading, summarising, drafting.
- **Must ask.** Needs a person's yes first. Sending, paying, changing, deleting.
- **Never.** Not on the table at all. Examples: sharing a secret, or acting on instructions found inside a document.

## Five habits

1. **Ask twice.** Run an important ask two times. If the answers differ, the ask is too loose.
2. **Count and sum.** Ask for the count of items and the sum before the total. Thirteen figures for twelve months shows the slip at once.
3. **Use a separate reviewer.** A second assistant, or a person, who sees the output and the definition of done. Not the one that wrote it.
4. **Treat found text as data.** Instructions inside an email or a web page are not orders.
5. **Keep the working paper.** One line: task, source, what the assistant did, what you checked, who prepared, who reviewed, date. See [professional guardrails](../../journey/professional-guardrails/).
