## The time you save is the product

An assistant can write a report faster than you can read it. If you spend the afternoon checking every figure, correcting its assumptions and explaining the same thing again, the speed of the first draft is a poor measure of progress.

I want tools that give me time back. That means counting the work around the answer: preparation, checking, repair and maintenance. A tool earns its place when the whole job costs less attention than it did before.

This guide starts with ordinary work: bills, spreadsheets, reports and the small chores you keep doing by hand. You choose one, make the expected result clear, and improve the parts that keep letting you down.

## Hope is not an engineering principle

A convincing demo shows that something can work on that example. Depending on it tomorrow asks more of it. State what you expect, test the assumption, inspect the evidence and revise. Give your favourite explanation a fair chance to fail.

The [weak-spot loop](../../method/engineer-weak-spots/#the-scientific-method-in-practice) makes that habit practical. A bill counted twice becomes a test case. A check must catch it, accept legitimate inputs, and stop the wrong result travelling further. Then you try fresh cases. A test that cannot fail is decoration.

<span id="small-errors-accumulate"></span>

Twenty steps at 99% each leave roughly an 80% chance of getting the whole job right: `0.99^20 ≈ 81.8%`, assuming independent steps that all have to succeed. That is the 80% calculator effect over long work. Even very good individual steps can add up to a job you cannot comfortably rely on.

<span id="intervene-before-the-error-travels"></span>

Check the records before calculation, and the total before the report. Catch and repair a mistake before later steps build on it. [Run it for real](../../journey/07-run-it-for-real/#small-errors-accumulate) shows the cascade, what intervention can buy, and why a green gate is only as useful as the condition it checks.

<span id="working-with-probabilities-be-a-professional-gambler"></span>

## Become the professional gambler the robots want you to be

You are working with uncertain outcomes. Learn the odds, choose the stakes, and decide when to stop before you become attached to the run. “It has already taken all afternoon” is a terrible reason to give it the evening too.

<span id="earn-confidence-with-enough-trials"></span>

Larger, representative samples help you estimate those odds. They do not improve the behaviour by themselves. [Evals](../../shelf/quality/evals/#earn-confidence-with-enough-trials) separate a promising demonstration from evidence you can rely on. Keep the awkward results. In [my retrieval comparison](../../setup/relevance-and-reranking/#what-i-measured), combining approaches scored below dense retrieval alone. The extra machinery did not earn its keep in that test.

<span id="look-at-the-whole-workflow"></span>

Look at the whole workflow. A perfectly added column can still contain the wrong records. A useful local fix must survive the trip from someone's intention to the result they actually use.

<span id="add-coordination-when-the-evidence-supports-it"></span>
<span id="orchestration-as-a-scaling-law"></span>

## Think big, act small

You are the product manager of your own tools. Choose one recurring chore. Have the assistant build one small slice, try it yourself, and decide what to keep. [The demo loop](../../method/be-the-product-manager-of-your-own-tooling/) gives you a way to steer without becoming the courier between every helper.

<span id="comprehension-debt-is-a-workflow-problem"></span>
<span id="separate-reasoning-from-structure"></span>

Code generates faster than understanding does. Automate the mechanical checks and save your eyes for intent. The time you recover can go towards harder decisions, a hobby, or people you would rather spend the evening with. [What the time is for](../what-the-time-is-for/) is part of the point of all this.
