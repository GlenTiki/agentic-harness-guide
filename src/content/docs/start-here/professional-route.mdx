If you already practise a profession, start from this: the assistant is a fast, fallible junior. It drafts quickly and it makes mistakes. Its output stays yours. This route keeps the pages that help with that and skips the ones that do not.

No page here replaces your organisation's AI policy. Read the policy first. If it forbids something this guide shows, the policy wins.

## The route, in order

1. [Level 0: Hello, robot](../../journey/00-hello-robot/). Skip the Scene if you wish. Do the Try it: it shows a confident total that is wrong.
2. [Level 1: Say it properly](../../journey/01-say-it-properly/). Bring your own standard. If you have a style guide, a policy or a checklist, paste it as the limits.
3. [Professional guardrails](../../journey/professional-guardrails/). Confidentiality, policy, sign-off, what never to delegate, sampling and the working paper.
4. [Why it sounds sure](../../journey/why-it-sounds-sure/). Why an assistant sounds certain when it is wrong, with a two-prompt demonstration.
5. [Level 5: Check the work](../../journey/05-check-the-work/). Write a checkable done, and use a reviewer who is not the preparer.
6. [Spreadsheet Monday](../../workflows/spreadsheet-monday/). Let the assistant write the formula, then check the formula. Never ask it to add in prose.
7. [Small shop month-end](../../workflows/small-shop-month-end/). A worked month-end: reconciliation, cut-off, variance and sign-off.
8. [Review content](../../workflows/review-content/). Review writing against a brief.
9. [Research a decision](../../workflows/research-a-decision/). Sources, a comparison and a stated recommendation.
10. Levels [3](../../journey/03-give-it-hands/) and [4](../../journey/04-trust-but-verify/), when you connect systems.

## What to skip

On the reference shelf, skip hooks, worktree isolation, small pull requests, spec-driven development and test-first and gates. They are about software delivery. The ideas behind them, such as a separate reviewer and a written definition of done, are already in level 5.
