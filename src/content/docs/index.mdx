### A plausible update can still promise too much

Constructed example, written to illustrate the check. This is not a recorded assistant reply.

**Your notes:** “The pilot passed 12 sample rows. Full import not run. Friday release depends on security review, which is still open.”

**A draft worth rejecting:** “Import complete. We are releasing on Friday.”

The sentences are tidy. They have also promoted a small pilot into a completed import and a conditional target into a commitment. A useful check compares each claim with the notes. The corrected update keeps the pilot scope, the unrun import and the open review visible. [Try this with your own notes](./start-here/consistency-fast-track/#try-it-on-one-update).

## Why this guide exists

I want to make effective agentic engineering accessible to anyone with a job worth improving. This guide draws on frontier-lab engineering reports and community practice, then adds my experience, experiments and judgement. You should be able to try the method without first becoming a framework collector. [See the foundations, alternatives and where I take a position](./shelf/foundations/principles/#where-this-guide-fits).

**Hope is not an engineering principle.** A convincing answer is a reason to investigate, not a reason to stop checking. If every result needs a full manual audit, the assistant has moved the work rather than removed it.

The habit I want to teach is the scientific method applied to everyday work: state what you expect, test it, inspect the evidence, and revise. Give your favourite explanation a fair chance to fail. “It worked once” is a promising start. It is also a very tempting place to stop. Ask me how I know.

Take a holistic view of the job. The brief, source data, tools, checks and human decisions all affect the result. A correct calculation on the wrong records still gives you the wrong answer. The [case for a harness](./start-here/why-bother/#hope-is-not-an-engineering-principle) starts with that whole workflow.

## What the tests showed: a €133 bill counted twice

<details>
<summary>Read the recorded bill experiment and compare the totals</summary>

I gave an assistant 40 monthly bill amounts for 39 months and asked it to split the total three ways. In 20 of 20 isolated runs, the reply noticed that the count was one too many. In all 20, the first stated total included all 40 amounts. Some replies put the warning before that total. The warning and the wrong total can coexist.

Across the wider evaluation, 14 of 20 model-and-prompt setups did not produce the failure each test targeted. The [evidence page](./evidence/) gives every count and the limits of those tests.
