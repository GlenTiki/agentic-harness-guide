## From the trenches: why this guide exists

I wrote this guide out of frustration with AI demo hype and the messy reality of day-to-day work.

I am Glen Keane. Over the past decade, I have worked across high-throughput distributed systems and performance tooling (former Node.js core collaborator, co-maintainer of `autocannon` with Matteo Collina, nearForm, and Wayflyer). Today, I work forward-deployed at Brightbeam, building enterprise AI systems and claims automation for clients like AXA and Zurich.

When people use AI assistants for serious tasks, they hit the same wall: the **80% calculator trap**. An adding machine that calculates correctly 4 times out of 5 does not automate your work; it doubles your anxiety. If you have to babysit every word or line of output, you suffer review fatigue and end up rubber-stamping results you do not understand. That is how comprehension debt explodes.

The solution is not "prompt engineering", larger context windows, or proprietary SaaS lock-in. The solution is sound engineering discipline made accessible to everyone:

1. **Be the Product Manager of your own tools**: You define high-level intent, constraints, and acceptance criteria. You never micromanage raw output.
2. **Mechanical verification gates**: Never trust unverified claims. Use simple spreadsheet checks, validation scripts, or test runners that halt on error before sign-off. (For software engineers, we cover isolated worktrees and automated hooks).
3. **Disposable micro-tools**: When a routine friction point repeats, have your companion build a focused personal tool. If a tool works, you keep it. If it breaks or stops serving you, it costs almost nothing to throw it away and build the next one in an afternoon.

This guide teaches the mindset, practical habits, and mechanics of building with autonomously self-improving tooling. Simple, checkable, and 100% open source.

## The empirical evidence: the €133 duplicate invoice

When raw chat models total 40 invoice lines, they sound confident, quote exact sums, and praise the neat input. Yet across 20 repeated trials, unguided models consistently miscalculate or include duplicate charges without warning.
