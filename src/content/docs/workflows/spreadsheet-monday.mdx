## Scene

*Last time (level 1): you made a request clear enough to check.*

*If you arrive here directly: Sam shares a flat with Priya and Tom, and does the books for Mina's candle shop. The same slip comes back in several levels: a month counted twice, or a month missing.*

Monday morning. Sam pastes twelve months of shared bills into a chat and asks for the total. The assistant answers with a confident figure. It is the sum of thirteen numbers, because February is in the list twice. A person adding the column in a spreadsheet would have seen thirteen rows. An assistant adding in prose sees none of that.

## The idea

**Never ask the assistant to add. Ask it to write the formula, then let the spreadsheet add.** A formula is a rule you can read, run and check. A total in prose is a claim.

Three habits keep the Monday routine safe:

1. **Formula, not sum.** The assistant writes the formula. The spreadsheet computes it.
2. **A check cell.** One cell shows TRUE or FALSE, so a wrong total shows itself.
3. **Traceable commentary.** Any sentence about the numbers uses only figures in the sheet, each with its cell reference.

Anonymise first. The steps are in [how to anonymise a spreadsheet](../../journey/professional-guardrails/#how-to-anonymise-a-spreadsheet).

### Part 1: the flat's shared bills

Put the months in A2:A13 and the amounts in B2:B13.

| Month | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Jan | 212 |
| Feb | 198 |
| Mar | 187 |
| Apr | 164 |
| May | 151 |
| Jun | 142 |
| Jul | 139 |
| Aug | 141 |
| Sep | 158 |
| Oct | 183 |
| Nov | 205 |
| Dec | 240 |

Ask the assistant to write the formulas. Do not give it the numbers to add.

```text
My sheet has months in A2:A13 and amounts in B2:B13. Write spreadsheet formulas for these, one per line, and do not calculate any answers:
1. the total of the amounts;
2. the number of months listed;
3. the number of distinct months;
4. a cell that says OK if the two counts agree and REPEAT if they do not;
5. each person's share of the total split three ways, rounded to the cent;
6. Sam's share, which is the total minus the other two shares, so the three shares add to the total exactly.
```

An example reply, and the result each formula should give:

| Task | Formula | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total | `=SUM(B2:B13)` | 2,120 |
| Months listed | `=COUNTA(A2:A13)` | 12 |
| Distinct months | `=SUMPRODUCT(1/COUNTIF(A2:A13,A2:A13))` | 12 |
| Repeat flag | `=IF(COUNTA(A2:A13)=SUMPRODUCT(1/COUNTIF(A2:A13,A2:A13)),"OK","REPEAT")` | OK |
| Share for Priya or Tom | `=ROUND(B14/3,2)` | 706.67 |
| Sam's share | `=B14-2*ROUND(B14/3,2)` | 706.66 |

Here B14 holds the total. The rounding rule is the owner's. Round each share to the cent. If the three shares do not add to the total, change Sam's share by the difference and say so. Three shares of 706.67 would add to 2,120.01, so Sam's share is 706.66. The assistant applies the owner's rule. It does not choose one.

The distinct-months formula divides by zero if a cell in A2:A13 is blank, so fill every row first.

**Make the check fail.** Change A4 from Mar to Feb. The total stays 2,120, the count stays 12, the distinct count drops to 11, and the flag says REPEAT. Change A4 back. A check that cannot turn red proves nothing, so run this once.

### Part 2: the shop's September

Mina's September sales, with a label in column A, a type in column B and the amount in column C:

| Line | Type | Amount (VAT included) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Online orders, gross | Online | 5,400.00 |
| Refunds (three of 50.00) | Online | -150.00 |
| Market card takings | Market | 1,720.00 |
| Market cash takings | Market | 410.00 |

In C7, type Mina's own total from her sales report: 7380. Write its source beside it.

| Task | Formula | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total sales | `=SUM(C2:C5)` | 7,380.00 |
| Online, net of refunds | `=SUMIF(B2:B5,"Online",C2:C5)` | 5,250.00 |
| Market total | `=SUMIF(B2:B5,"Market",C2:C5)` | 2,130.00 |
| Check cell | `=ROUND(SUM(C2:C5)-C7,2)=0` | TRUE |

The check cell compares the sheet with Mina's total in C7, which she typed from her records. If the cell says FALSE, the sheet and Mina's records disagree, and you stop and find out why. The tolerance is zero.

Next, budget in column B, actual in column C, variance in D and percentage in E:

| Line | Budget | Actual | Variance | % |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Materials | 1,400.00 | 1,530.00 | `=C3-B3` gives 130.00 | `=D3/B3` gives 9.3% |
| Other costs | 100.00 | 112.00 | `=C8-B8` gives 12.00 | `=D8/B8` gives 12.0% |

Format column E as a percentage, or `=D3/B3` shows 0.0929 and not 9.3%. A positive variance on a cost is adverse (A). A positive variance on sales is favourable (F). In the full table, sales is row 2, materials row 3 and other costs row 8. A flag for lines past the investigation threshold: `=AND(ABS(D3)>50,ABS(E3)>0.05)`. The percentage is a fraction, so 5% is 0.05. It gives TRUE for materials (130.00 and 9.3%) and FALSE for other costs (12.00 is under 50.00). The rule, and why it uses "and", are on the [small shop month-end](../small-shop-month-end/#step-5-budget-against-actual) page.

### Part 3: the commentary

```text
Using only the figures in the table below, write two sentences on the materials variance. Quote each figure with its cell reference, for example "actual 1,530.00 (C3)". Write "Reason needed from Mina" where a reason would go. Do not suggest a reason.

[paste the table]
```

Check every figure in the reply against its cell. A figure with no cell is an invented figure.

Before you paste real figures from a real business, anonymise them and read [confidentiality and sign-off](../../journey/professional-guardrails/). Definition of done: the formulas give the results above, the check cell shows TRUE, every figure in the commentary has a cell reference, and the work is reviewed and approved by a named person who is not the preparer, or by the fallback reviewer in [using an assistant at work](../../journey/professional-guardrails/).

## Under the bonnet

*How it works. Optional reading.*

An assistant produces text one piece at a time. When it adds a long list "in its head", it writes a likely-looking total, and a likely total can be wrong. A formula moves the arithmetic to a tool that does it the same way every time.

Asking for the formula also gives you something to read. You can see that the range is A2:A13 and not A2:A12. You can test the formula on a case where you know the answer. That is a check you can run, and a total in prose is not.

The check cell turns the rule into a signal. A person skimming a sheet will not notice a wrong total, but will notice a red FALSE.

:::note[At work]
Keep the check cell, the source figures and the reviewer's initials on the sheet. That is a working paper: it shows where each number came from and who checked it. A total typed over a formula, or a range that stops one row short, are the usual ways a sheet goes wrong.
:::

## Expert corner

:::caution[Expert corner]
The distinct-count trick treats "Feb" and "Feb " (with a trailing space) as different months, and "Feb" and "feb" as the same one. Clean the column first. Floating-point sums can leave tiny differences, so compare money with a tolerance, as in the check cell, not with exact equality.

An assistant can write a formula that looks right and refers to the wrong range. Test every formula on a case with a known answer before you trust it on the real data.

Anonymising reduces what leaves your machine. It does not remove the need for permission to use the data.
:::

## Agent note

:::note[Agent note]
**Topic: spreadsheet arithmetic.**

State a figure only if it came from supplied data, a cited source or a computation you show. When you total numbers, state how many you added and the sum. Do not list the items unless asked and there are 50 or fewer.

When asked for a total, give the formula and say what range it uses. Do not report a result you did not compute with a tool.

Quote each figure in commentary with its cell reference. Do not supply a reason for a variance.

Check: the reply states how many figures were added and the sum, and every figure in the commentary has a cell reference.
:::

## Mistakes people make

**Asking the assistant to add.** It returns a tidy total with no way to inspect it. Fix: ask for the formula, run it in the sheet and compare.

**Never making the check fail.** A check that has only ever shown TRUE may be checking nothing. Fix: break the data on purpose once, and watch the cell change.

**Trusting commentary with loose figures.** A sentence says materials rose "about 130" and nobody can find the cell. Fix: require a cell reference for each figure.

## Checkpoint

<p class="audience"><em>Audience: the reader. Agents may skip the quiz.</em></p>

You can now:

- Ask an assistant for a formula instead of a total.
- Build a check cell that shows TRUE or FALSE, and make it fail on purpose.
- Ask for commentary in which every figure points at a cell.
- Anonymise a range before you paste it.

**Self-test**

1. The repeat flag says REPEAT. What do you do first?
2. Why does Sam's share use the total minus the other two shares?
3. A commentary sentence says "costs rose 14%". The table has no 14%. What do you do?

<details>
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Look for a month listed twice. Count the rows, then find the duplicate. Fix the data, not the flag.
2. Three shares of 706.67 would add to 2,120.01. Taking Sam's share as the total minus the other two makes the shares add to the total, as the rounding rule requires.
3. Treat it as an invented figure. Remove it, or ask for the calculation and cell references.

</details>
