I want more people to get useful work from an assistant without first becoming experts in the machinery behind it. That means starting with work people recognise: a budget, a message, a decision or a recurring chore.

The skill I want this guide to teach is judgement. You decide what matters. You give the assistant a task. You doubt an answer enough to check it, then revise the work when the check finds a problem. Confidence in the wording is not evidence that the work is right.

## A guide we can correct

The [Guide](../../journey/) offers a sequence of exercises. These Field notes make room for questions, experiments and corrections as the guide develops. I want to learn in public and make it possible for readers to challenge the work.

A useful explanation must survive contact with the person who needs it. Can you understand the next step? Can you try it with a task you know? Can you tell whether the result meets your needs? Those questions matter as much as the technical machinery.

## The foundations belong to many people

This guide builds on research and documentation from the labs that develop these systems, and on practitioners who share their experiments. The [reference shelf](../../shelf/foundations/principles/) links to sources behind the methods. Those foundations deserve credit. My contribution here is to explain the ideas, connect them to everyday work and make the checks possible to try.

Start with [one small task](../../start-here/consistency-fast-track/). Keep the part that helps. Question the part that does not.
